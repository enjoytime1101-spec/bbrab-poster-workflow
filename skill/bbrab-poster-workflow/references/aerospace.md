# Aerospace production rules

- `rocket`: launch vehicle / launch service. Check silhouette, proportions, stage and fairing boundaries against client references; distinguish liftoff, separation and orbital scenes.
- `satellite`: satellite hardware. Check body, arrays and antennas against the identified model; do not assume every satellite has the same structure.
- `services`: remote sensing, communications or other satellite applications. Show actual business value; do not substitute a rocket image for the application. Illustrative coverage or imagery must not imply measured performance.

`concept` means labeled fiction. `real_product` requires model_name, evidence_source and a product file. Do not reconstruct unseen hardware. Use the supplied cutout or photograph and design around it. Lack of a usable reference is a blocker, not permission to invent one.

Additional human checks: vehicle_geometry, flight_context, mission_claims, reference_fidelity, holiday_hierarchy. Evidence must refer to visible regions and a reference; any unknown blocks a passing judgment. Engineering review and customer acceptance are not automated.

Conceptual reading only: [NASA satellite overview](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/satellite/en/) and [NASA rocket systems](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/rocket-parts/). These are not references for a customer's specific model. No NASA assets or logos are bundled.
